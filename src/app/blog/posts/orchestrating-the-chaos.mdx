When we think of a film festival, our minds immediately go to the glaring screens, the red carpets, and the thought-provoking discussions. But behind every successful event lies an invisible engine keeping everything running. Recently, I had the privilege of stepping away from my IDEs and code editors to be part of that engine, volunteering for 5 days as part of the Catering Coordinator Division at the Purbalingga Film Festival.

At first glance, managing catering might seem worlds apart from Informatics Engineering. However, over the course of those five intense days, I realized that the core principles are surprisingly identical: it is all about resource management, precise timing, and troubleshooting on the fly.

My primary responsibility was to ensure that hundreds of committee members, volunteers, and guests received their meals on time and in the right quantities. In the tech world, we call this load balancing and database management. If a server goes down, an application crashes; if the catering arrives late or the distribution is chaotic, the entire festival's mood and schedule can collapse.

Coordinating the logistics of food distribution required me to build a mental framework similar to developing a digital inventory system. I had to track what was coming in, what was going out, and anticipate peak hours. When unexpected changes occurred—like a sudden shift in the event rundown or a delay in delivery—my team and I had to "debug" the situation instantly, finding alternative solutions without causing panic. It was a masterclass in agile problem-solving.

But beyond the logistical challenges, this role was deeply human. Food is a source of comfort, especially for a crew working long, exhausting shifts. Ensuring that everyone was well-fed and taken care of trained my empathy in a very direct way. It reinforced my belief that whether you are designing a user interface (UI) for a web application or handing out lunch boxes, the end goal is exactly the same: providing a seamless, satisfying experience for the "user."

Those five days at the Purbalingga Film Festival were physically demanding, but they sharpened my perspective. The experience taught me that the best systems—whether digital or human—are built on a foundation of solid coordination, adaptability, and a genuine desire to serve others. I carry these lessons with me as I continue to build tech solutions that are not only functionally robust but also deeply human-centric.
